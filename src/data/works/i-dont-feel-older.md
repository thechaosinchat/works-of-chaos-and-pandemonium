title: "I Don’t Feel Older."
created: 2026-05-14
edited: 2026-08-05
---

I don’t feel older.

This celebration, if you wish to call it that, is absolutely abysmal.

I’m now 4 years away from being able to purchase alcohol in the United States, a privilege “adults” have.

We grow older. Another year gone.

We grow older just to realize the time we’ve lost.

We grow older just wishing to go back, wishing time slows down, wishing you could’ve enjoyed everything just a little more.

We grow older just to realize that you’ve discarded too much time on pointless activities: instead of playing video games until 3 am, you could’ve been enjoying time with friends, or applying for colleges. Yet you did it anyway. And probably will again if you could.

We grow older just to realize the childhood we’ve lost.

We grow older just to realize that growing up sucks, that we’d rather be children, without a care in the world, playing with friends that you can acquire like coins on a sidewalk that you just can’t find when you’re older.

We grow older just to realize that other people suck. They trick, manipulate, and waste your time, all the while treating you like you are inferior, like they are the apex predator even though you are just the same.

We grow older just to realize that teachers and parents are just as human, living through life with just as much emotion and pain as you are. They listen to music and watch movies and shows just like you, and they’ve learned the same lessons you have without them.

We grow older just to realize the pain of adulthood.

We grow older just to realize that maturing doesn’t simply come with age or puberty, but a careful concoction of motivation and time.

We grow older just to realize that that motivation doesn’t come to small-you so easily.

We grow older just to realize that adulthood doesn’t mean you can enjoy ice-cream for breakfast, play on your Ipad for the entire day, and stay up late.

We grow older just to realize that adulthood means working long hours, scrounging for cash to support family, trying to find fun in the little things, and coming home exhausted to find your girlfriend of 9 months missing, probably sleeping with another dude.

We grow older just to realize that… we don’t want to grow older.

We grow older just to realize age is just the timer of Thanatos, like a bomb strapped to your chest, ticking and ticking through each ticking second.

We grow older just to realize time is truly of the essence.

The essence of life and the essence of joy.
